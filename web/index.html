Eidolon

A planning tool for Earth analog sites. Pick a site on the Moon or Mars: Eidolon ranks the places on Earth whose terrain form comes closest at 200 m, then helps plan a test campaign at one. On the Moon, matching reaches 59.5° from the equator for now.

Every rover, drill and habitat bound for a Moon base or Mars rehearses on Earth first. Eidolon finds where.

How it works

  1. Pick a siteA candidate base location on the Moon, a landing site on Mars, any named feature, or a point you pin.
  2. Read its terrainThe search reads the terrain form of an 18.8 km window at 200 m: landform shape and size, relief, slope, roughness, raised or incised. Features under about 600 m across don’t show at this scale.
  3. Find its doublesEarth’s land is ranked by how closely its terrain form matches. Typically 2 of 3 searches find a good terrain analog in the first five results. On the Moon, matching reaches 59.5° from the equator for now, so the south-pole base regions show terrain only. Compare side by side, then plan a test campaign.

Beyond the Moon and Mars

Venus, Mercury and the outer moons ask for habitats Earth can only partly rehearse. Eidolon starts with the Moon and Mars.

Venus

Cloud cities

At about 50 km up, the air is near 1 bar and around 75 °C. Habitats float in the clouds instead of landing, as in NASA's HAVOC study.

Concept
Mercury

Polar mines

Permanently shadowed craters near the poles hold water ice, a short traverse from ground that reaches 430 °C by day.

Concept
Callisto, Jupiter

Low-radiation base

Far enough out to miss Jupiter's worst radiation. NASA's HOPE study picked it as the site for a crewed outpost in the Jupiter system.

Concept
Titan, Saturn

Shoreline habitat

Nitrogen air at 1.5 bar and seas of liquid methane. No pressure suit needed, only warmth and oxygen at −179 °C.
